Aadhaar Biometric Authentication Failures

A case of algorithmic and system bias in national infrastructure. Welfare services in India, such as PDS rations, old-age pensions, MGNREGA wages and AePS banking, often require a fingerprint or iris match before a person receives what they are entitled to. When the match fails, a genuine beneficiary can be sent home empty-handed.

This platform uses a simulated log of authentication attempts to ask a simple question: who carries the cost when biometric authentication fails? Elderly people, manual labourers and people in rural or remote areas with poor devices or connectivity are expected to be hit hardest. The pages below measure whether, and how much.

How one visit works

1. ScanThe device captures a fingerprint or iris and scores its quality (0-100).
2. MatchThe scan is compared with the stored template. A score below the threshold is rejected.
3. RetryUp to three attempts are allowed in one visit.
4. OutcomeIf all attempts fail, the service is denied for that visit.

Key distinction: FRR measures how often a genuine person is wrongly rejected; FAR measures how often an impostor is wrongly accepted. A strict system keeps FAR low but pushes FRR up, and the cost of FRR falls on the people the welfare scheme is meant to serve.
