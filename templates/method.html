Method, assumptions, limits

Every finding here was built in on purpose

The disparities come from documented mechanisms in the simulation, listed below. They are not measurements of the real Aadhaar system. The point is to show how such mechanisms become measurable exclusion, and how standard fairness tests catch it.

How the data was generated

A seeded generator (data/generate_aadhaar_auth_dataset.py) invents {{ kpis.total_beneficiaries|thousands }} beneficiaries. Each makes {{ assumptions.visits_per_beneficiary }} visits with up to {{ assumptions.max_attempts_per_visit }} attempts: {{ kpis.total_visits|thousands }} visits and {{ kpis.total_attempts|thousands }} attempts. For every attempt it:

  1. Computes capture quality (0–100) from occupation, device, conditions and age, plus noise. Iris quality ignores occupation, conditions and age.
  2. Checks for a network timeout, then a device error. Either ends the attempt with no match score.
  3. Computes a match score that rises with quality for genuine users and stays low for impostors.
  4. Rejects quality below {{ assumptions.min_capture_quality }} as poor capture, and scores below {{ "%.2f"|format(threshold) }} as mismatch.

The original generator was unavailable, so this one is a reconstruction calibrated to the supplied CSV. The analysis always uses the original CSV unchanged.

Ethics and risk lens

The matcher has no bug: it applies one threshold to everyone, exactly as designed. The harm is an ethical failure, not a technical one, so it is read against the four core principles of responsible AI.

PrincipleWhat goes wrong hereWhat this study does
FairnessWorks on average ({{ kpis.visit_denial_rate_genuine|pct }} of genuine visits denied) but fails the elderly, manual workers and remote areas far more oftenMeasures denial per group and tests it with the four-fifths rule and chi-square
AccountabilityWhen a pensioner is turned away, no one answers for it; “the machine said no”Recommends fallbacks, manual override and on-the-spot grievance redressal
TransparencyA denied person is not told whether it was poor capture, a mismatch or the networkSeparates biometric from system failures; documents every assumption
PrivacyFingerprints and iris scans are sensitive, directly identifying personal dataUses synthetic data only; collects and stores nothing personal

Where bias enters the pipeline

  1. Collect · measurement bias. Scanners capture worn or dusty fingers poorly; manual work costs 12 to 20 quality points.
  2. Set the objective · evaluation bias. One threshold ({{ "%.2f"|format(threshold) }}) is tuned for security: few impostors get through, many genuine users are rejected.
  3. Deploy · deployment bias. Remote and rural areas are given low-quality devices and poor networks more often.
  4. Impact · disparate impact. The elderly and manual workers are denied many times more often.
  5. Feedback · hidden exclusion. People who give up after repeated denials never appear in the logs.

Privacy and governance

Under India's Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, our mapping of the roles:

  • Data principal: the beneficiary whose fingerprint is scanned.
  • Data fiduciary: UIDAI and the department running the scheme.
  • Data processor: authentication agencies and device operators.

A privacy impact assessment of authentication: identify the data (biometrics, highly sensitive), assess the risk (exclusion of the most vulnerable), mitigate (the recommendations below) and document (publish denial rates and the four-fifths audit).

Limitations

  • Synthetic data. Every effect size was chosen by the modeller.
  • Assumed bias is found bias. The analysis recovers the mechanisms put in; it cannot discover new ones.
  • Small groups. People aged 75+ and iris users are small samples, so their rates are less precise.
  • Simplified matching. Real systems fuse several fingers and offer OTP fallbacks.
  • No time dimension. Upgrades, outages and seasons are not modelled.
  • Model. Effects are additive on the log-odds; within-person correlation is ignored.

Recommendations

  • Fallback authentication. OTP, iris or a documented manual override, so no one leaves without their entitlement.
  • Better devices first where they are worst. Replace low-quality scanners in rural and remote areas.
  • Offline mode. Authenticate locally when the network is down and reconcile later.
  • Exemptions. Alternative proof for people aged 75+ and manual workers with worn fingerprints.
  • Grievance redressal. On-the-spot help and a stated reason for every denial.
  • Monitoring. Publish denial rates by age, occupation and area, with the four-fifths check.
